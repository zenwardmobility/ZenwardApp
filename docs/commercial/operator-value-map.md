# Zenward — Operator Value Map

**Purpose:** connect each real, demonstrable Zenward capability to the operational pain it addresses for a small-to-mid-size NEMT operator. Used to prepare a demo for a specific operator's priorities, and to keep the sales conversation grounded in problems Zenward actually solves today — not a generic feature list.

Every row's "Zenward capability" is AVAILABLE NOW or LIMITED-PILOT per `product-capability-matrix.md` — nothing aspirational is listed as solved.

| Operator pain | Today, without Zenward | Zenward capability | Where it's shown in the demo |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I don't know what's going wrong until a facility calls to complain." | Dispatcher finds out reactively — a phone call, a missed pickup discovered after the fact. | Today's Operations surfaces unassigned trips, open issues, and stale location signals proactively, the moment you log in. | 10-min demo, Step 1 |
| "Assigning drivers is a whiteboard, a group text, or a phone call — and it's easy to double-book." | Manual coordination, no system-enforced conflict prevention. | One dialog to assign a driver + vehicle; concurrency-protected so two dispatchers can't silently clobber the same trip. | 10-min demo, Step 2 |
| "I have no idea if a driver actually left, or if 'en route' is just what someone told me." | Status is whatever the last phone call said — often stale or simply assumed. | Every lifecycle step is a driver-initiated, server-recorded action — status reflects reality, not assumption. | 10-min demo, Step 3 |
| "When a driver goes quiet, I don't know if that's normal or a problem." | No visibility between "assigned" and "arrived" — a black box. | A location-freshness signal shows dispatch when the driver's device last checked in, and visibly ages if it goes stale. | 10-min demo, Step 4 |
| "When something goes wrong on a trip, it lives in someone's memory or a text thread, not anywhere I can find later." | Ad hoc — a text message, a sticky note, nothing searchable. | Report/resolve issues inside the trip's own record, preserved permanently, never deleted. | 10-min demo, Step 5 |
| "If a family member or a facility asks 'what actually happened,' I have to reconstruct it from memory." | Manual reconstruction from multiple sources, if it's even possible. | A full, timestamped activity trail on every trip — assigned, en route, issue flagged, issue resolved, arrived, completed. | 10-min demo, Step 6 |
| "My passenger notes, referring facilities, drivers, and vehicles live in three different spreadsheets." | Fragmented records, easy to lose sync. | Passengers, Facilities, Drivers, and Fleet all live in one system, feeding the same trips every other screen already shows. | 15-min demo, master-data tour |

## Where Zenward is honest about not being the whole answer yet

An operator evaluating Zenward against a bigger incumbent will notice gaps — say so proactively rather than let them discover it mid-demo:

- No broker/payer integration yet — trips are entered directly, not pulled from a broker feed.
- No billing/invoicing yet.
- No live map or route optimization — location is a freshness signal, not navigation.
- Adding new facilities, vehicles, and driver accounts happens with the Zenward team during onboarding today, not self-serve.

Frame these as "here's exactly where we are, and here's what a founding-pilot partnership looks like" — see `founding-operator-program.md` — rather than glossing over them.
