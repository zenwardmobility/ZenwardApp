# Zenward — Operator Objections & Responses

**Purpose:** honest, prepared responses to the objections a Zenward team member is most likely to hear from an NEMT operator. Every response stays inside `sales-claims-boundary.md` — none of these promise something not yet true.

---

**"We already use [Excel / a group chat / a whiteboard] and it works fine."**
That's exactly the gap Zenward closes — not because a whiteboard can't work, but because it doesn't scale past a certain number of trips or drivers, and it leaves no record when something goes wrong or a facility asks what happened. Ask: "How do you currently find out a driver hasn't left yet, or that a trip needs a different vehicle? How long does that take today?" Let their own answer make the case.

**"We already use [a larger incumbent dispatch platform]."**
Don't disparage a specific competitor. Ask what's working and what isn't — Zenward is not trying to be a like-for-like replacement of a mature, broker-integrated platform today; it's a focused, modern operations layer, and honest about not yet having broker integration or billing (see `product-capability-matrix.md`). If their real pain is visibility, assignment speed, or accountability, that's exactly where Zenward is strong today.

**"Does this integrate with our Medicaid broker?"**
No, not yet — be direct. "Not today. Trips are entered directly rather than pulled from a broker feed. That's a real gap for an operator who gets most of their volume through broker dispatch, and we're upfront about it." Do not imply a roadmap date unless one is genuinely committed and documented.

**"Is this HIPAA compliant?"**
"We haven't completed a HIPAA compliance assessment, so I can't say we're HIPAA compliant today. What I can tell you is exactly how the system enforces data isolation and access control" — then walk through `security-overview.md`. Never round this up. See `sales-claims-boundary.md`.

**"How much does this cost?"**
Refer to the founding operator program rather than quoting a number from memory: "We're running a limited founding-operator pilot with discounted pricing for early partners — let me walk you through what that looks like." See `founding-operator-program.md`. Never hard-code or improvise a price.

**"Can drivers use this without a company phone / without installing anything?"**
Yes — it's a mobile web app in the driver's own browser, no native app install required. Be honest that it needs the driver's phone to have a working browser and enough signal to load pages and submit updates; it has no offline mode.

**"Does this track my drivers all the time?"**
No — and be emphatic here, because this is the objection most likely to come from a driver, not an owner. "Location sharing is per-trip, driver-initiated by tapping a button, and only active while that specific trip is in progress. There's no background tracking outside a trip, ever." This is also a genuine product limitation worth being upfront about: it means dispatch has no visibility into a driver between trips.

**"What happens if the internet or the system goes down mid-trip?"**
Be honest: there is no offline mode today. A driver needs connectivity to advance a trip's status or share location. This is a real limitation for rural/low-signal service areas — say so rather than deflect, and note it as a known gap, not a hidden one.

**"Who else is using this?"**
Do not name any operator without their explicit, current written permission (see `sales-claims-boundary.md`). If no operator can currently be named, say so plainly: "We're working with our first founding partners now — you'd be joining at the ground floor, with direct input into what we build next," and pivot to `founding-operator-program.md`.

**"This looks new / early — how do I know it'll be supported?"**
Acknowledge it directly rather than oversell maturity: "You're right that this is an early-stage product. That's exactly why the founding operator program exists — close collaboration, fast iteration on real feedback, and pricing that reflects the partnership, not a mature-product price for an early-stage product." Let the founding program's structure (documented, time-bound, real check-ins) be the answer, not a vague reassurance.
